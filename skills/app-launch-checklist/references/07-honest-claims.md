# Part 7 — Honest claims and transparency

## 27 Unsupported claims — *Depends on jurisdiction · Get qualified advice*
Objective claims (results, speed, savings, security, AI capability, competitor comparisons) need evidence *before* publishing (FTC Advertising Substantiation policy; EU/UK misleading actions/omissions; UK CAP/BCAP codes). Health, financial, environmental and AI-capability claims get extra scrutiny. "Guaranteed to double your revenue" needs proof; "Track every sale in one place" just needs to be a real feature.
**Audit:** list every claim across site, app, store listing, ads, emails, sales materials; classify as factual, comparative, guarantee, statistic or puffery.
**Substantiate:** dated evidence for every objective claim; statistics state source, sample and date where it matters; remove/rewrite unsupported claims; qualify typical-results claims.
**Maintain:** re-check after product changes; remove outdated claims.

## 28 Fake reviews — *Depends on jurisdiction*
FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, in force **October 2024**); UK DMCCA bans fake reviews (in force **6 April 2025**); EU UCPD bans fake reviews and requires saying whether/how reviews are checked. Removing fabricated reviews doesn't mean removing genuine ones.
**Audit:** trace every review, rating and testimonial to a real customer and source; identify staff/friend/AI/paid and incentivised reviews.
**Fix:** remove untraceable reviews; disclose incentives/relationships clearly next to the review; treat negative and positive genuine reviews by the same rules; describe review collection/checking where EU/UK require.
**Maintain:** never buy reviews or reward only positive ones.

## 29 False SOC 2 claims — *Good practice · Depends on jurisdiction*
SOC 2 (AICPA) is an independent attestation by a licensed CPA firm against the Trust Services Criteria — Type 1 (point in time) or Type 2 (over a period). **It's not a certification**, licence or universal requirement. "SOC 2 certified/compliant" is misleading if no report exists, it's out of date, or it covers a different system; false claims can be deceptive (FTC Act) and breach contracts. Having no SOC 2 report is fine — claiming one you don't have isn't.
**Audit:** search site, app, sales decks, security questionnaires and trust pages for SOC 2, ISO, HIPAA, GDPR and similar wording; match each to a current report/certificate, scope, type and period.
**Fix:** remove unsupported claims; describe status precisely ("SOC 2 Type 2 report available under NDA for the period…") not "certified"; don't imply a cloud provider's report covers your app; describe actual practices without unearned labels.
**Maintain:** review whenever a report expires or scope changes.

## 30 Realistic uptime commitments — *Good practice · Get qualified advice*
No general law sets uptime levels, but contractual promises are binding and untrue availability claims can be misleading. "100% uptime, guaranteed" is rarely achievable.
**Set:** base targets on measured history and providers' SLAs; define measurement, period and covered components; list exclusions (announced maintenance, customer-caused issues, force majeure); define remedies (service credits) and how to claim, if offering an SLA.
**Communicate:** remove "100% uptime"/"never goes down"; honest status page for incidents and maintenance.
**Verify:** independent monitoring compared with the commitment monthly.

## 31 Customer logos only with permission — *Good practice · Depends on jurisdiction · Get qualified advice*
"Trusted by…" logos imply a relationship and often endorsement; logos are trademarks and contracts often restrict publicity. Risks: trademark/false endorsement (US Lanham Act §43(a)), contract breach, deceptive advertising (FTC Act, Endorsement Guides).
**Check:** list every logo, company name and case study on site, app, sales deck and store listing; check each contract for publicity/confidentiality/logo clauses.
**Act:** written permission per logo (incl. brand guidelines and approved wording); remove non-customers or non-permitted logos; avoid wording implying endorsement/partnership/certification when it's only a customer; keep permissions on file with date and scope.
**Maintain:** remove promptly when the relationship ends or permission is withdrawn.

## 32 AI-generated content labels — *Depends on jurisdiction · Depends on your app*
EU AI Act **Article 50** transparency obligations began applying **2 August 2026**: providers must make clear when people interact with an AI system and mark synthetic audio/image/video/text in a machine-readable way (reports indicate systems already on the EU market before that date have until **2 December 2026** for marking). Deployers must disclose deepfakes and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest, subject to exceptions. Elsewhere mainly good practice, platform policy, or avoiding deception. Check the latest European Commission guidance — details are still being clarified.
**Map:** every place the app generates/edits text, images, audio or video, and every chatbot/agent; your role per feature under the AI Act (provider, deployer, neither), with advice if unclear.
**Implement:** tell users clearly when they're talking to AI; visible labels on AI-generated/materially edited images, video, audio where users could be misled; machine-readable marking (metadata, content credentials) where role and market require; keep labels on download/share/embed where feasible; follow store and social platform AI-disclosure rules.
**Verify:** labels appear on every surface incl. exports and notifications.
